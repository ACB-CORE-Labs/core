## 0. Why this is one stack

Two ADRs, one decision, split across a design/implementation seam that the corpus uses often: **ADR-0091 defines a manifest-metadata contract; ADR-0093 is the wiring ADR that exists only because 0091 shipped as schema-without-enforcement.** ADR-0093 declares `Depends on: ADR-0091, ADR-0092`, restates 0091's nine predicates as its scope, and adds nothing to the contract itself — no new field, no new status tier ("No new capability status tier beyond ADR-0091's five"). They are not readable apart: 0091's Validation Semantics list is inert prose until 0093 names the module that evaluates it, and 0093's "Items" list is unreadable without 0091's field table.

The stack was commissioned to answer one cross-reference question, so it is worth stating the answer at the top and then proving it:

> **This stack does not bear on `AA-111`.** ADR-0091/0093 governs *language packs* in `packs/data/<pack_id>/manifest.json` — the compiled lexicon/gloss packs — via **optional** manifest fields read by `packs/domain_contract.py`. `AA-111`'s six loaders govern *behavior packs* in `packs/<family>/<pack_id>.json` — flat, uncompiled JSON with `.mastery_report.json` seals. Two disjoint artifact families, two disjoint directories, two disjoint ratification mechanisms, **zero shared imports in either direction**. Option **(c)**, with the sharp edge that the word "domain pack" is what made the question worth asking (§7.3).

## 1. Stack-level claim

> **A language pack may only affect a capability-ledger status claim by declaring, in its own manifest, machine-checkable links to the chains, eval lanes, reviewers, and open gaps that back the claim — and a generated validator, not the pack, decides whether those links hold.**

ADR-0093 states a named, falsifiable invariant. It is treated FA-1 style.

- **Pre-registered criterion** (`domain_contract_v1_predicates_enforced`, ADR-0093 §Invariant, verbatim): "for every pack with `domain_contract_version: 1`, running domain-contract validation either passes all nine ADR-0091 predicates or emits a typed error naming each failing predicate. **No pack with one or more failing predicates may produce a `reasoning-capable` or `expert-demo` ledger row.**"

- **Verdict: the first sentence is GO, the second is NO-GO.**
  - *First sentence — the validator itself works.* All seven contract-bearing packs evaluate; math/logic returns nine typed `PredicateResult` rows with per-predicate notes (`transitive=8, proof_chain=8, contradiction=8`), and the negative cases in `evals/domain_contract_validation/runner.py` show each predicate failing on a minimally broken input. This half is honestly built.
  - *Second sentence — the enforcement clause is not implemented anywhere.* `core/capability/reporting.py:32` imports `validate_domain_contract_pack` (the **structural parser**) and nothing else from this stack; `evaluate_domain_contract` — the function that runs the nine predicates — has **zero callers outside `core/cli_capability.py:58` (an operator-invoked dry-run subcommand), the eval-lane runner, and three test files** (`rg -n "evaluate_domain_contract"`, whole tree). #### 1. Content summary

- **Decision made:** extend language-pack manifests with nine **optional** fields (`domain_contract_version`, `domain_id`, `axioms`, `rules`, `teaching_chains`, `eval_lanes`, `reviewers`, `known_gaps`, `provenance`) that a pack must supply only when it claims `reasoning-capable` or `expert-demo` status; define nine validation predicates over those fields; and rule that **the generated ledger owns status** — manifest fields supply evidence and links, never an asserted maturity level.
- **Alternatives explicitly rejected:** (i) requiring the reasoning fields globally — "Cognition, relations, register, identity, safety, and many lexical packs legitimately do not carry formal axiom sets. Requiring those fields globally would create schema churn without adding reasoning evidence"; (ii) letting a pack's existence promote a domain — "It does not promote a domain because a pack exists"; (iii) hand-asserted maturity — "Manifest fields provide evidence and links; they do not manually assert maturity"; (iv) validator-side mutation — "No validator may mutate a pack. Mutation remains a reviewed proposal flow."
- **Artifacts the ADR claims will exist:**
  - the nine optional manifest fields, with the required/optional split per the §Decision table
  - nine validation predicates (§Validation Semantics 1–9)
  - five capability statuses with predicates (`seeded`, `grounded`, `reasoning-capable`, `expert-demo`, `blocked`) owned by the generated ledger
  - `domain_id` resolves against "a known ledger domain"
  - `reviewers` resolve through `docs/reviewers.yaml`
  - `known_gaps` resolve through `docs/gaps.md`
  - the no-mutation rule
  - (per its own Acceptance evidence block) `packs/domain_contract.py`, `core/capability/domain_contract_predicates.py`, `tests/test_capability_cli.py`, `tests/test_adr_0097_mathematics_logic_ratification.py`, `tests/test_adr_0100_0102_sibling_ratifications.py`

#### 3. Liveness / integration

The **parser** half of this ADR is genuinely live. `validate_domain_contract_pack` has three non-test call sites, all reached without an operator flag:

1. `core/capability/reporting.py:393-396` — every `ledger_report()` call builds a `domain_contracts` array per domain. `core capability ledger` is a shipping command and the ledger is cited across the ADR corpus as the promotion surface.
2. `workbench/logos.py:517-520`, consumed at `:166-171` — Logos Studio turns `contract.valid` into a `SafetyVerdict.CLEAR` / `.FAILED` badge, typed through `workbench/schemas.py:850-851` and rendered at `workbench-ui/src/app/logos/LogosRoute.tsx:452-454`.
3. `core/cli_capability.py:51-56` — the `--structural-only` branch of the dry-run subcommand.

It is **not** on the chat/serving path: no `chat/`, `generate/`, or `field/` module imports it, and a turn never consults a domain contract.

**Sabotage test.** Stub `parse_domain_contract` to return `DomainContractValidation(pack_id, present=False, valid=True)` unconditionally:
- **Would change:** the `domain_contracts` array on every ledger row empties out, so `tests/test_adr_0097_…::test_provenance_points_at_adr_0097` (which asserts an `adr-0097:reviewed:` provenance string reaches the ledger row) fails, and the Logos Studio badge flips to `FAILED` for all packs.
- **Would not change:** any domain's `status`. Not one. The ledger's `seeded`/`grounded`/`reasoning_capable`/`audit_passed`/`expert` predicates are computed at `reporting.py:407-434` and `:453-492` from `_pack_metrics`, `chain_inventory`, a hardcoded holdout path, and `evaluate_expert_demo` — the contract's `valid` flag is never read.

So the parser is live as a **reporting and display** surface, and dead as a **gating** surface. That is a weaker liveness than ADR-0091 §"Validation Semantics" reads ("A domain pack can affect capability claims only if…"): today a domain pack does not affect capability claims through this path at all — it affects the *display of evidence about* those claims.

**Liveness axis:** **live** (as reporting/display: three unconditional call sites, one of them user-visible) — with the explicit caveat that the gating role the ADR's own §Validation Semantics language implies is carried entirely by ADR-0093's evaluator, which is `wired-but-unreached` (see that card).

#### 5. Build fidelity — does the code match the decision?

Three divergences, all in the same direction (the code is weaker than the text), plus one supersession:

1. **P2 lost half its content.** ADR-0091 §Validation Semantics 2: "Gloss checksum **and definitional closure** pass for packs that provide glosses or definitions." `_predicate_p2_gloss_closure` (`…/domain_contract_predicates.py:141-194`) computes one SHA-256 and compares it. No closure check exists — `rg "closure"` across `core/capability/` and `packs/` returns only `versor_closure_rate` (unrelated, ADR-0106) and `packs/schema.py:68`'s comment about ADR-0084's *proposed*, dormant definitional layer. The function's own name and docstring still say "closure". Worse, the surviving half is redundant: `packs/compiler.py:450-464` already verifies `glosses_checksum` inside `load_pack`, and P1 *calls* `load_pack` — so a gloss-checksum mismatch raises inside P1 and P2 can never be the predicate that catches it.
2. **P3 cannot fail.** `packs/domain_contract.py:16-22` `_KNOWN_DOMAIN_IDS` and `core/capability/domains.py:3-14` `DOMAIN_PACKS.keys()` are the same five strings, hardcoded twice. The parser appends `scope_boundary:domain_id:unknown` for anything outside the first set (`packs/domain_contract.py:128-129`), which makes `validation.valid` false, which makes `evaluate_domain_contract` return at `:509-518` with `predicates=()`. `_predicate_p3_domain_known` is therefore only ever reached when it is guaranteed to pass. The eval lane's own `p3_unknown_domain` case knows this — it asserts on `report.contract_errors`, not on P3's `passed` (`evals/domain_contract_validation/runner.py:187-195`).
3. **P4 lost "read-only".** ADR-0091 §Validation Semantics 4 requires corpora be "registered **and read-only**". `_predicate_p4_chains_registered` checks set membership only. The module docstring at `…:14` even paraphrases the requirement down to "Chain coverage (P4/P5/P6) consults `chain_report`", quietly dropping it.
4. **The status vocabulary moved and ADR-0091 didn't.** ADR-0091's §Capability Status table names five statuses ending in `expert-demo`. `reporting.py:37-39` has six, and `expert-demo` is emitted nowhere. ADR-0113 renamed it and ADR-0120 added `expert` — a clean supersession *in substance*. But ADR-0113's own acceptance-evidence list claims "README, **ADR-0091..0112 narrative refs**, runtime_contracts, capability_roadmap updated to the new vocabulary" (`ADR-0113:152-154`), and ADR-0091 was not updated. A record that says it was reconciled, and wasn't.

**Build-fidelity axis:** **partial drift** — the schema and the ledger-owns-status rule match exactly; three of the nine predicates (P2, P3, P4) are weaker in code than in text, two of them unfalsifiably so; one status table is stale against a supersession that claimed to have fixed it.

#### 7. Necessity / generality

1. **Necessity.** Genuinely necessary *as a decision*. Remove ADR-0091 and there is no mechanism by which a pack can name the evidence backing a capability claim in a machine-checkable way; the ledger would be reduced to counting lemmas and chains, and promotion would revert to reviewer assertion. The ADR is doing real work the system has no other way to do. What is *not* necessary is the specific nine-item list: two of the nine (P2, P3) are unfalsifiable in the current implementation (§5), so the honest predicate count is seven.
2. **Reducibility.** No L0/L1 operator does this. This is a governance/evidence-linking contract over JSON metadata — the algebra layer (`algebra/`, `field/`) has no analogue and cannot acquire one; there is nothing here for a rotor or a geometric product to absorb. Distinguish this cleanly from `22-consolidation-report.md` Clusters 1 and 2, where the narrow mechanism *was* re-deriving an existing L0 operator.
3. **Extensibility — the commissioned question, answered.** Could this contract, generalized slightly, absorb `AA-111`'s six loaders (`packs/identity/loader.py` 494, `packs/register/loader.py` 608, `packs/rhetorical_style/loader.py` 425, `packs/anchor_lens/loader.py` 422, `packs/ethics/loader.py` 409, `packs/safety/loader.py` 259)? **No — and not "not yet"; structurally no.** The evidence, all verified by reading the code rather than the prose:

   | | ADR-0091/0093 domain packs | `AA-111`'s six behavior packs |
   |---|---|---|
   | Artifact shape | a **directory** `packs/data/<id>/` with `manifest.json` + `lexicon.jsonl` + `glosses.jsonl`, compiled to a `VocabManifold` | a **single flat file** `packs/<family>/<id>.json`, never compiled |
   | Loader | `packs/compiler.py::load_pack` (`:531`), one shared implementation for all language packs | six independent loaders, one per family |
   | Search paths | none — `_load_pack_cached` hardcodes `Path(__file__).parent/"data"/pack_id` (`packs/compiler.py:434`) | `_resolve_search_paths` + `_find_pack` in each of the six (e.g. `packs/safety/loader.py:105-124`) |
   | Ratification | reviewer registry (ADR-0092) + gap registry + eval-lane splits | `<id>.mastery_report.json` self-seal via `formation.hashing.verify_seal` |
   | Ratification bypass | none | `CORE_ALLOW_UNRATIFIED_{IDENTITY,SAFETY,ETHICS,REGISTER,ANCHOR_LENS,RHETORICAL_STYLE}` — six env vars, verified present |
   | Error discipline | **result object** (`DomainContractValidation`, errors as a tuple of strings) — no exception class | six bespoke exception classes (`SafetyPackError`, `IdentityPackError`, …), fail-closed raise |
   | Cross-imports | — | **zero**: `rg "domain_contract\|DomainPackContract" packs/{identity,safety,ethics,register,anchor_lens,rhetorical_style}` → no matches; `packs/compiler.py` and `packs/schema.py` likewise do not import `domain_contract` |

   Beyond the mechanics, the *semantics* don't transfer: the contract's substance is P4–P7 (teaching corpora, ≥8 reviewed chains per operator family, ≥3 intent shapes, dev/public/holdout splits). None of those concepts has a referent for a safety pack, whose content is a list of boundary ids. And `_KNOWN_DOMAIN_IDS` is a closed five-element set of *reasoning* domains — an identity pack has no member of it to declare.

   **So: option (c).** ADR-0091/0093 governs a different kind of pack entirely, does not resolve `AA-111`, and does not add to it either — notably, `packs/domain_contract.py` is **not** a seventh instance of the duplicated skeleton: it has no `_resolve_search_paths`, no `_find_pack`, no env-var bypass, and no error class, and its result-object error discipline is the *better* design of the two. `AA-111`'s general `PackLoader[T]` still does not exist anywhere in the tree.

   One partial-scope caveat worth recording, because it is what makes (c) non-obvious: ADR-0091's §Decision prose explicitly names "Cognition, relations, **register, identity, safety**, and many lexical packs" as packs that "legitimately do not carry formal axiom sets," which reads as though those families are inside the contract's optional scope. They are not inside its *reach*: `validate_domain_contract_pack` resolves `root / pack_id / "manifest.json"` under `packs/data/` (`packs/domain_contract.py:192-200`), and `packs/identity/`, `packs/safety/`, `packs/register/` contain no `manifest.json` at all. For those three families the contract is not "optional" — it is structurally unreachable, and calling it optional in the ADR text is what invites the (a)/(b) reading.

   The real extensibility pairing is *internal* to this stack and points the other way: see the ADR-0093 card §7.

**Necessity/generality axis:** **irreducible** — no L0/L1 operator, and no other ADR's construction, provides evidence-linked capability gating for language packs; the decision earns its place. Two riders: (i) the nine-predicate list is not itself irreducible (P2 and P3 are unfalsifiable and should be either repaired or retired under Axiom 7), and (ii) the missing conjugate direction (§4, Axiom 4 / G-23) is a hole in this ADR's own design, not an argument for consolidating it into something else.

3. **Extensibility.** The consolidation candidate is entirely internal to this stack and points **inward**: `ledger_report` should call `evaluate_domain_contract` (with the `chain_inventory`/`reviewer_registry` injection points that already exist precisely for a multi-pack ledger pass, `…:480-487`) and delete its inline re-derivation, which would simultaneously (i) implement ADR-0093's Invariant, (ii) make ADR-0113's item-1 claim true, (iii) remove the weaker duplicate of P1/P2/P7, and (iv) give P4/P8/P9 gating force they have never had.    The `AA-111` pairing does **not** apply here — see the ADR-0091 card §7.3 for the disjointness evidence.

**Necessity/generality axis:** **generalization-candidate** — one mechanism (the nine predicates) exists in two implementations at the same layer, the stronger one unwired and the weaker one deciding licences. Consolidate *toward* `evaluate_domain_contract`, not away from it.

## 3. Stack-level synthesis

**Internal consistency.** The two ADRs agree with each other completely — 0093 adds no field, no tier, no semantics 0091 didn't specify, and its "what is deliberately not in scope" section correctly preserves 0091's optionality rule. There is no place where the later ADR silently contradicts the earlier. The unreconciled contradiction in this stack is not *internal*; it is between ADR-0093's Invariant and the ledger code, and between ADR-0113's restatement of that Invariant and `expert_demo.py`. That is a healthier failure than the reverse — the design is coherent, the wiring is one edge short.

**Cumulative build state.** Unusually high for a two-ADR stack, and the shortfall is concentrated in one place. Every manifest field: built. All nine predicates: built and named. All five ADR-0093 Items: built. The dry-run CLI, the lane, the SHA pin, the reviewer and gap integrations: built. What is not built is the single edge from `ledger_report` to `evaluate_domain_contract` — one import and one call. So the stack is ~90% built by artifact count and 0% built on the axis its own Invariant names. This is the inverse of the usual "looks healthy ADR-by-ADR, stalled halfway" pattern the template warns about: it stalled at the *last* step, which is why every intermediate artifact looks green.

**Cumulative necessity/generality read.** One coherent generalizable mechanism, not N narrow ones — ADR-0091's "packs declare falsifiable links, a generator adjudicates" is a single idea, cleanly stated, correctly implemented, and it has no duplicate anywhere else in the corpus. The consolidation opportunity is *within* the stack (§7.2 of the ADR-0093 card: the ledger's inline re-derivation of five predicates plus a second `docs/gaps.md` parser), and it resolves in the same motion as the enforcement gap — wiring `evaluate_domain_contract` into `ledger_report` deletes the duplicate and implements the Invariant with one change. That is the rare consolidation that is also a repair.

**On the commissioned question.** For `22-consolidation-report.md` Cluster 3: **this stack is not `AA-111`'s missing general contract, and it does not add to `AA-111` either.** Option (c), with receipts in the ADR-0091 card §7.3. Two consequences for the consolidation report: (i) the recommendation to build a generic `PackLoader[T]` stands unchanged and unassisted — nothing in the tree can be promoted into that role, so it is net-new work; (ii) a *prerequisite* to that work is now visible — the term "domain pack" refers to two disjoint artifact families (ADR-0033/0044's ethics-domain packs; ADR-0091's language-domain packs), and any consolidation ADR written without disambiguating it will be misread the way this stack's own title was.

**Blast radius if this stack's central claim is wrong.** The claim under audit is "a language pack may only affect a capability-ledger status claim by declaring machine-checked evidence links." It is not *wrong* — it is *unenforced*. The cascade is therefore about which downstream claims currently rest on enforcement that does not happen:
